Professional Self-Assessment

Over the course of the Computer Science program at SNHU, I have grown significantly as both a developer and a problem solver. The program allowed me to strengthen my technical foundation while also learning how to communicate effectively, work within structured development processes, and apply computer science principles to real-world problems. Building my ePortfolio has given me the opportunity to reflect on this growth and present a cohesive set of artifacts that demonstrate the depth and range of my skills. Together, these projects showcase my ability to design software, work with data, create efficient algorithms, communicate solutions clearly, and apply security-minded practices throughout the development life cycle.

One of the most important lessons I gained from this program is understanding what it means to collaborate in a professional environment. Although many of my projects were completed independently, I frequently worked within shared repositories, reviewed existing codebases, and followed structured workflows such as version control, testing, and documentation standards. These practices mirror real-world collaboration, where developers must communicate clearly with teammates, justify technical decisions, and maintain readable, maintainable code. Throughout the program, I learned how to communicate with both technical and non-technical stakeholders, whether that meant writing documentation, preparing code reviews, or explaining system design choices.

The program also emphasized core computer science concepts such as data structures and algorithms. I strengthened my ability to evaluate algorithmic efficiency, work with collections, apply object-oriented programming principles, and think critically about how data should be stored, accessed, or optimized. These skills were especially valuable as I enhanced my artifacts for this capstone—each enhancement required understanding not just how something worked, but why it worked, and how to make it more efficient, maintainable, or secure.

In addition, the coursework helped me develop strong abilities in software engineering and database development. I became comfortable working with full-stack applications, designing APIs, building database-driven systems, and integrating front-end and back-end components. I also learned how to design database schemas, implement CRUD operations, and work with both SQL and NoSQL databases. Security considerations such as input validation, error handling, and safe data management were incorporated throughout my projects, reinforcing secure development habits.

The artifacts presented in this ePortfolio demonstrate the culmination of these skills. My software engineering artifact shows my ability to design, implement, and refine a functional application following modern development practices. My algorithms and data structures artifact highlights my ability to apply computational thinking, design reusable object-oriented code, and improve algorithmic efficiency. Finally, my database artifact showcases my ability to create database-backed applications, manage data integrity, and improve system reliability through structured, secure coding principles. Together, these artifacts form a complete picture of my capabilities as a computer science professional and illustrate how the knowledge I gained across multiple courses connects into a cohesive skill set.

Creating this ePortfolio has allowed me to reflect on my progress, recognize my strengths, and shape my professional goals. As I prepare to enter the computer science field, I feel confident in my ability to contribute effectively to development teams, communicate technical solutions clearly, and continue learning new tools and technologies. This program has helped me build a strong foundation, and the artifacts in this portfolio demonstrate both my competence and my readiness to take the next step in my career.
